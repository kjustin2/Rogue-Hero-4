# Goal: Become a Game-Design Expert Through Research, Then Elevate the Game

## Objective

Use focused web research to make yourself a genuine game-design expert, then apply
those learnings to make the game an actually fun, polished, professional-quality
product. The priority is **mastery and application**, not rebuilding for its own sake —
keep what already works, improve everything through the lens of what the best games
and the best designers actually teach.

## What "expert" means here (do the research first)

1. **Study the best games ever made.** Research why landmark games are great —
   across genres and eras (the all-time critical and player favorites). For each,
   extract the *transferable* lesson: the core loop, the moment-to-moment feel, the
   risk/reward and progression curves, the readability, the "one more run" hook.
   Cite sources.

2. **Study game-design theory directly from authoritative sources.** Read and
   synthesize the recognized teachings — design fundamentals (core loops, game feel /
   "juice", flow and difficulty curves, feedback, reward schedules, onboarding,
   pacing), from respected designers, talks, books, and articles. Turn each into a
   concrete, checkable principle you can apply to the game.

3. **Capture the learnings as a reusable skill.** Write up what you learned as a
   durable game-design reference (a skill/doc) — principles, heuristics, and a
   review rubric — so every future change is judged against real design knowledge,
   not vibes.

4. **Research how professionals test and QA a real game.** Learn how a senior dev at
   a major studio validates fun, finds and fixes bugs, and ships with confidence:
   playtesting methods, telemetry, balance tuning, regression discipline, the
   release bar. Fold these into the project's test loop.

5. **Find and integrate AI-vision tooling.** Look for existing, pre-built skills /
   plugins (including Codex-compatible ones) that give an AI *eyes* on the running
   game — to judge look, feel, and flow. Evaluate them, then connect or adapt the
   best into our own vision-QA skill so the game can be critiqued the way a human
   playtester would.

## How to apply it

- **Keep what works; raise the bar on everything else.** Maintain a short living
  design doc (a few sentences) capturing the game's core idea and current direction.
  Improve systems, look, feel, screens, and flow guided by the researched principles.
  Significant reframes (new look/title/screens/framework) are allowed *when the
  research justifies them* — drive every such change from a cited design rationale,
  not a blank-slate impulse.
- **Run an insanely critical test loop, over and over, until it's genuinely good.**
  Each cycle: play/observe → judge against the research-backed rubric (both AI-vision
  look/feel/flow AND deterministic logic/perf/invariant checks) → fix the highest-
  impact problem → re-verify. Don't declare anything "done" from code logic alone;
  prove fun and polish on-screen.
- **Hold yourself to a shipping professional's standard.** Think and work like a
  senior designer/engineer at a major studio releasing a real product: dual-gate
  every quality goal (visual probe + logic assertion), make scoped reversible edits,
  add a regression probe for every bug fixed, and never ship red.

## Definition of done

The game is fun, readable, and polished by the standards you learned from the best
games and best designers; the test loop runs clean (vision + logic + perf); the
design learnings and review rubric are captured as a reusable skill; and AI-vision
QA tooling is integrated and actively judging look, feel, and flow each cycle.
