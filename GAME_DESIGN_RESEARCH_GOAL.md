/goal Study the Greatest Games Ever Made, Distill the Craft, Encode It in Skills

## Objective

Become a top-tier, all-around game-design and game-engineering expert through deep
web research into the best games of all time and the teachings of the best designers.
Distill what you learn into durable, reusable skills — and sharpen our existing skills
with the same learnings — so every future decision is judged against real craft, not
guesswork. Keep all learnings **general across genres**, not roguelike-specific.

## What to research

1. **The best games ever made — across every genre.** Study widely and deeply: the
   all-time critical and player favorites in action, RPG, platformer, shooter,
   strategy, puzzle, roguelite, sim, and more. For each, extract the *transferable*
   lesson — core loop, moment-to-moment feel, risk/reward and progression curves,
   readability, pacing, the "one more turn" hook. Cite sources.

2. **The teachings of the best designers.** Read and synthesize the recognized canon
   — design fundamentals, game feel / "juice", interesting decisions, flow and
   difficulty curves, feedback, reward schedules, onboarding, and pacing — from
   respected designers, talks, books, and articles. Turn each into a concrete,
   checkable principle.

3. **Code organization, structure, and systems of great games.** Study how the best
   games are *engineered* — architecture, state management, update/render flow, data
   layout, systems design, and the patterns that keep them maintainable and fast.
   **Look at real code examples**, but stay relevant to our stack and style
   (browser games: Vite + TypeScript, Three.js / Canvas2D+WebGL2, procedural art,
   synthesized audio) — **not heavyweight engines like Unreal**.

4. **Best techniques across the board.** Mechanics, balance, juice, screen feel, and
   the small techniques that separate pro work from amateur work. Learn what the best
   actually do, and why.

5. **Menus, flow, and structure to industry standard.** Study how the best games
   handle menus, navigation, settings, options, tutorials/onboarding, accessibility,
   and overall flow. Distill the standards so our games have proper, professional
   menus, settings, and tutorials — not afterthoughts.

6. **Pro-level testing and AI-driven QA.** Learn how senior studio professionals
   validate fun, find/fix bugs, and ship with confidence — playtesting, telemetry,
   balance tuning, regression discipline, performance gating. Crucially, level up our
   ability to let **AI learn the flow of a game and *see* its visuals** — to judge
   look and feel, check performance, and drive concrete improvements each cycle.

## What to produce

- **New reusable skills, where applicable.** Capture the distilled craft as durable
  skills (principles, heuristics, review rubrics) we can apply on every project —
  general, genre-spanning, and actionable.
- **Upgraded existing skills.** Fold the learnings back into the skills we already
  have to make them materially better (design rubric, vision/QA loop, render-look,
  perf, audio, ship, etc.) — don't let new knowledge sit in a doc when it belongs in
  a skill that's used every session.
- **Cited rationale throughout.** Every principle ties back to a source — a named
  game, designer, talk, book, or article — so it's knowledge, not opinion.

## Definition of done

You can reason about any gameplay, feel, balance, onboarding, menu/flow, or
architecture decision at a professional level, citing real games and real teachings;
the learnings live in reusable skills (new and upgraded); and our AI testing/QA can
learn a game's flow, see its visuals, judge look-and-feel and performance, and turn
that into measurable improvements.
